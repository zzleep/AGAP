# Taal Volcanic Ashfall Protection Guide

> **Regional Vulnerability**: Due to Santa Rosa's proximity to Taal Volcano (~35km north-northwest), prevailing southerly winds can deposit heavy volcanic ashfall across the city.

## 1. Personal & Respiratory Protection
- **Wear N95 Masks**: Use N95 or KN95 respirators outdoors to filter fine silica ash particles. If masks are unavailable, use a damp cloth or handkerchief.
- **Eye Protection**: Wear protective goggles or glasses. Avoid wearing contact lenses as ash particles cause severe corneal abrasions.
- **Long Sleeves & Footwear**: Wear full-coverage clothing to prevent skin irritation from acidic volcanic ash.

## 2. Residential Home & Water Protection
- **Seal Windows & Doors**: Close all windows, doors, and air vents. Place damp towels along door gaps to block fine ash infiltration.
- **Protect Drinking Water**: Cover open water storage drums (pails, tanks, deep wells) to prevent heavy metal and acidic contamination.
- **Turn Off Air Conditioners**: Shut off split-type and window AC units to avoid drawing abrasive ash into cooling coils.

## 3. Roof Ash Accumulation Clearing
- **Prevent Roof Collapse**: Volcanic ash is extremely heavy, especially when wet. Clear roofs promptly using a broom or scraper once ashfall subsides.
- **Safety Harness**: Wear non-slip shoes and safety lines when clearing roof G.I. sheets.
- **Dampen Ash Before Sweeping**: Lightly spray ash with water mist before sweeping to prevent re-suspending hazardous dust particles.

## 4. Vehicle & Driving Caution
- **Avoid Unnecessary Travel**: Ashfall reduces visibility to near zero and makes asphalt roads as slippery as ice.
- **Air Filter Cleaning**: Check and clean car air intake filters after driving through ashfall areas.
